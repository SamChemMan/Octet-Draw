# Octet Draw 1.1.2

This update fixes problems you reported and adds features you asked for.

## Highlights

- When you minimise a spectrum, it shows as a pill beside its structure. Click the pill to expand the spectrum again.
- To predict a ¹⁹F or ³¹P NMR spectrum, choose Structure > Predict ¹⁹F-NMR Shifts or Predict ³¹P-NMR Shifts.
- The template library has 15 new sets and 228 new templates, such as lab equipment, cells and a Jablonski diagram.
- The colour menus are the same in each panel. To get a colour from the screen, click Pick from screen.
- In the stoichiometry table, you can set a column as a catalyst or a solvent. Octet makes one table for each step of a scheme.
- On Windows, a drawing that you paste into Word is an Octet Drawing object. Double-click the object to open the drawing in Octet.

## Spectra

- When you minimise a spectrum, it shows as a pill beside its structure. Click the pill to expand the spectrum again.
- When you open a spectrum, Octet minimises the Actions panel. When you open the Actions panel, Octet minimises the spectrum.
- To minimise the spectrum or to expand it again, you can also choose View > Show or Hide Spectrum.
- The spectrum panel and the Mass spec panel have three controls at the top right: Minimise, Full screen and Close.
- Each structure has its own spectra, as tabs in one panel. To add a spectrum, click + and choose it. To close a spectrum, point at its tab and click the X.
- If you close a spectrum by mistake, choose Undo before you change the drawing. The spectrum comes back as it was.
- To open the Mass spec panel, choose Structure > Show Mass Spec Analysis. You can also click Mass spec analysis in the Actions panel.
- To change the height of a spectrum, drag its top edge.
- To move from peak to peak, press Tab until a peak has the focus. Then press the arrow keys. A screen reader reads each peak.

## NMR

- To predict a ¹⁹F or ³¹P spectrum, choose Structure > Predict ¹⁹F-NMR Shifts or Predict ³¹P-NMR Shifts.
- ¹H and ¹³C spectra show the splitting from fluorine and phosphorus.
- A ¹⁹F spectrum shows its ¹H couplings by default. A ³¹P spectrum does not. To show or hide the ¹H couplings, click Decouple ¹H in the spectrum panel.
- Octet opens files of measured ¹⁹F and ³¹P spectra. If a file does not give the nucleus, Octet finds the nucleus from the frequency.
- Predicted ¹H and ¹³C shifts are more accurate near sulfonyl groups, in heteroaromatic rings and in bridged bicyclic rings.
- When you click Details in a ¹H spectrum, the Quality column shows Rough for OH, NH and SH shifts. The solvent changes these shifts.

## Templates

- Eight new sets are clip art: Lab equipment, Biomolecules, Cells, BioArt, Anatomy, Organisms, Bugs and Symbols.
- Seven new sets are structures: Side chains, Peptides, DNA and RNA, Supramolecules, Polyhedra, Carbon and Photochemistry.
- The Photochemistry set has 17 templates, such as a Jablonski diagram, a light arrow (hν), singlet oxygen and a sensitiser. To change one part of the Jablonski diagram, double-click that part.
- The Lab set has 29 more items, such as a three-neck flask and a Dean-Stark trap.
- In the template library, click Structures or Clip art to show the sets of that kind. Your own sets show with both.
- When you search the template library, Octet shows the results in two groups: Structures and Clip art.
- With the new sets, Octet starts as fast as before.

## Drawing

- The colour menus in the Actions panel, in Settings and on Home are the same. Each colour menu has recent colours, presets, your colours, Pick from screen and Add colour…, in this order.
- To get a colour from the screen, click Pick from screen.
- To change the order of your colours, drag them. To rename or remove a colour, right-click it.
- To give part of a chemical label its own colour, select the characters and then choose a colour.
- When you choose Colour > By Element a second time, Octet removes the element colours.
- To remove one hydrogen from an atom, point at the atom and press the key of its element. For example, press O on OH to get O.
- When you select one bond alone, such as ethane, the Actions panel shows the structure actions and the formula. The Actions panel does the same for one labelled atom alone, such as H₂O.
- To open the periodic table, hold the Text tool. Click an element, then click an atom to change it to that element. In the periodic table, point at an element to see its atomic weight and its isotopes.
- To fade atoms in a 3D structure that has depth shading, select the atoms. Then move the Opacity slider in the Actions panel.
- Octet draws each small fishhook arrow and each small curved arrow as one smooth curve. The arrowhead follows the curve, also in exports.
- When you change a structure, Octet also changes the values that you added from the Properties panel. To keep one of these values as it is, type new text in its caption.
- In Settings > Keyboard shortcuts, you can set a key for each circled charge, positive and negative.
- Add multi-centre attachment and Add variable attachment have new icons.
- When you point at a folded section in the Actions panel, it opens. To close it, click its title one time.
- When a button such as Cancel has the focus in a dialog, Return presses that button.
- When you drag a box over drawings on Home, Octet selects them. Click Remove to remove them from Home or from the open folder.
- When you choose Structure > Clean Up Reaction, Octet also turns each product to the same orientation as its starting material. To keep the products as you drew them, choose Edit > Undo one time.
- Octet no longer has Augmented Clean Up Reaction. Clean Up Reaction does the same work.
- On the Mac, pinch on the trackpad to zoom the drawing. You do not have to hold a key.
- In a table from the Table tool, press Shift+Return to move up one cell.

## Stoichiometry table

- Each reactant column has a role: Reactant, Catalyst or Solvent.
- To change the role, click a cell in the column. Then choose Set as reactant, Set as catalyst or Set as solvent in the Actions panel.
- In a new table, Octet gives the Solvent role to each common solvent above or below the arrow, such as THF.
- For a catalyst, the Equivalents row shows mol%. Type 5 or 5% to set 5 mol%.
- When a scheme has two or more arrows, Structure > Analyse Stoichiometry makes one table for each step.
- When you type the Measured mass of a product, Octet sets the amount of that compound in the next step.
- When a column has the Solvent role, the Concentration row shows the concentration of the limiting reactant in the solvents. For example, for 1 mmol in 5 mL of THF, the Concentration row shows 0.20 M.
- When you type a concentration for a solvent, such as 0.1 M, Octet calculates the volume of that solvent.
- Reactants have Density and Volume rows. The Density row shows the density of 41 common liquids, such as THF, DCM and triethylamine.
- When you type a volume for one of these liquids, Octet calculates the mass and the moles.
- When you type the Expected mass of a product, Octet calculates the amounts of the starting materials.
- When you type a Mass or a Volume for a reactant, Octet marks that amount as measured. The mark is red text with a red line under it. To add or remove the mark, click the cell and choose Mark as measured or Clear measured mark.
- To type the Temperature, Time, Atmosphere and Other conditions, click a cell and choose Conditions… in the Actions panel. Octet shows the conditions to the right of the table.
- The table includes more of the reagents above and below the arrow, such as THF, cat. H2SO4 and K2CO3 (2 eq.).
- The table also includes ions and words, such as H+, OH−, proton and water.
- A coefficient in a scheme, such as the 2 in 2 A + B → C, sets the equivalents. A fraction, such as ½ O2 or 1/2 O2, does the same.
- The table calculates the molar mass of formulae with 27 more elements, such as Mo, Ba, Ce and Bi.
- When you type a mass with no unit, Octet uses grams. To use milligrams, set Mass without a unit to Milligrams in Settings > Keyboard shortcuts.
- To copy the table as text, click a cell and choose Copy table in the Actions panel. You can paste the text into a spreadsheet or a document.
- To hide each row that has no value, click a cell and choose Hide empty rows. To show these rows again, choose Show all rows and columns.
- To move between cells, click a cell and then press an arrow key or Tab.
- To type in a cell, press Return. To replace the value in the cell, type a character.
- When you press Delete in a cell, Octet removes the value that you typed. It does not remove the table.

## Chemistry

- Convert Structure to Name names more ring systems, such as galantamine, brefeldin A, cefotaxime and the calixarenes.
- The Sequence tool draws peptides, RNA and DNA with a straight backbone. To give the same backbone to a chain that you drew, choose Structure > Clean Up Structure.
- When you paste HELM or FASTA text, Octet puts the sequence in the field of the Sequence tool. Press Return to draw the sequence.
- To copy a peptide, RNA or DNA chain as text, choose Edit > Copy As > HELM or FASTA.
- Choose Structure > Expand Generic Structure to SDF… to save each structure that a generic structure stands for. Octet saves up to 10,000 of these structures in one SD file.
- The Properties panel has a CAS number row. It shows the number of each structure that is in Octet's dictionary. If Online search is on, Octet gets the other CAS numbers from PubChem.

## Files

- In Settings > Document > Properties, you can set the title, the author and the keywords of a drawing. Octet saves these properties in the file and in exported PDFs.
- When you make a new drawing, its Author is your name from your computer account.
- Choose File > Insert… to add a saved drawing or a picture to the open drawing. You can also drop the file on the drawing.
- Choose File > Apply Settings From File… to give the open drawing the settings of a ChemDraw style sheet (.cds).
- Choose Settings > Document > Save to file… to save the settings of the open drawing as a .cds file.
- To show File > More Formats, set Settings > Pro > More formats to on. Pro is a new part of Settings.
- From File > More Formats, you can export CML, BMP and 3MF files. You can also import and export connection tables (.ct) and copy SLN.
- Export 3MF… makes a ball-and-stick model of your structures for a 3D printer.
- From File > More Formats, you can open crystal structures (CIF) and the ligands in mmCIF files. Octet keeps their 3D shape as depth.
- Export LaTeX (chemfig)… saves the structures as chemfig code in a .tex file. The rings, wedges, charges and labels in the chemfig code are the same as on the page.
- To set the bond length, the line widths and the fonts of some objects only, select the objects. Then change the values in Settings > Pro > Selection.
- To give these settings to different objects, click Copy settings. Then select those objects and click Paste settings.

## Search

- The Search menu has three new commands: Search SciFinder, Search Google Scholar and Search Google Patents.
- Search SciFinder opens SciFinder and copies the SMILES of the structure. Paste the SMILES into SciFinder.
- Search Google Scholar searches for the name of each structure. Search Google Patents searches for the structure by its InChIKey.
- When you click Find hazards in the Properties panel, Octet shows the GHS hazards of each structure from PubChem. The hazards are the pictograms, the signal word, the hazard statements and the precautionary codes. Find hazards shows only when Online search is on.

## Windows, Linux and Word

- On Windows, a drawing that you paste into Word, PowerPoint or Excel is an Octet Drawing object. Double-click the object to open the drawing in Octet.
- To paste a picture only, set Settings > Import and export > Copy > Office object to off.
- On Windows, when you paste a drawing into Word, the picture is its true size at 100% scale. In 1.1.1, a drawing with labels was 7.5 times too large at 100% scale.
- On Windows and Linux, the Window menu lists the open drawings. A tick shows the drawing that is in front.
- On Linux, when you choose File > Print… or File > Print Selection…, Octet shows the pages first. You can see where the pages break before you print.
- On Linux, each release build makes a Flatpak test version of Octet. It helps us find why drawing is slow on some NVIDIA computers.

## Fixes

- When you make many edits in a large drawing, Octet no longer stops.
- If Octet cannot calculate a formula, the Actions panel shows a dash.
- If Octet cannot load its chemistry engine, a notice tells you to restart Octet.
- A 3D structure with many identical groups, such as triisopropylsilyl groups, no longer stops Octet for minutes.
- When you zoom in, pen curves and clip art no longer show thin gaps along their lines.
- Each colour menu shows its colours at full strength, so Black no longer looks grey.
